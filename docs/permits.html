Which permit

The full Residential Addition / Alteration permit, not the simple kitchen remodel one. Plano's Bathroom / Kitchen Remodel Scope of Work Permit excludes "the removal of any bearing wall or partition" and any change to load-bearing floor, wall, ceiling or roof elements, and this job takes out a load bearing wall for the island (Sequence, steps 11 to 13).

For that permit, the city's Additions / Alterations checklist and Customer's Guide ask for:

  • The Residential Permit Application, which names the owner and lists the general, electrical, plumbing and mechanical contractors and the trash hauler on one form.
  • Two copies of construction documents, drawn to scale and dimensioned: floor plans showing the existing and adjoining rooms, framing details, the engineer's structural drawings for the beam and its posts and footings, and electrical and plumbing plans (outlet spacing and lighting; these may be on the floor plan).
  • The plan review fee and a deposit of 65% of the building permit fee, paid up front. The rest is paid before the permit is issued.

Residential plan review takes about 5 working days per round of comments. A permit lapses if work doesn't start within 180 days of issue, or stops for 180 days. Every permit needs a final inspection.

Questions

  1. Owner as general contractor. May I act as the owner-GC on my homestead, and under what conditions? Do I need the $100 general contractor registration, and must I live in the house?
  2. Permits. Is this the Residential Addition / Alteration permit rather than the kitchen remodel scope of work permit? Do the electrical, plumbing and mechanical work go on that permit through each listed contractor, or as separate trade permits? Is a demolition permit needed for the interior tear-out? If the wall removal company pulls a permit for the beam, how does it fit with mine?
  3. Licensed trades. Must each trade be licensed and registered with Plano? May I do any electrical or plumbing work myself? Is an AAV allowed at the island sink?
  4. Drawings. What drawings, structural documents and equipment details must be submitted (for example the hood's spec sheet and CFM, the appliance circuits, the beam calculations)? Who may prepare them: may I draw the floor plans myself, and must the beam, posts and footings be sealed by a Texas licensed engineer? Do the island wall's slab anchoring and the counter brackets need the engineer too?
  5. Inspections. Which inspections happen before work is covered (footings, the beam, underground conduit and drain in the slab trench, framing, rough electrical, plumbing and mechanical)? Who schedules each: me in eTRAKiT or the trade? What completes the final closeout?
  6. Fees. Is the fee schedule effective 2026-01-02 still current? Is the $0.48 per sq ft charged on the kitchen's area or the house's (Square footage)?
  7. Review time. Is residential plan review still about 5 working days per round?
  8. Expiration. How long does a submitted application stay open before it expires? Does the permit still lapse at 180 days without work or without an inspection?

Square footage

The fee schedule doesn't say what area the $0.48 per square foot applies to for an alteration. The application has a separate "Square Footage" box under "Complete this Section for Alterations/Additions", apart from the house's total, which suggests the area of the work: the kitchen, not the house. Only a new house is clearly charged on its full area ("under roof"). This is a reading of the forms, so confirm it with the city.

The fee is small either way. A 200 sq ft kitchen comes to $96 for the building permit, plus $45 plan review and $45 each for electrical, plumbing and mechanical (each is $0.01 per sq ft with a $45 minimum): about $276, before any re-inspections. Charged on a 2,500 sq ft house, the building permit alone would be $1,200. The 200 sq ft is a placeholder, since the room hasn't been measured.
