Trade sequence

Draft order of work from demolition to move-in, with the owner as general contractor, 2026-11-04 · index

Written from the plan, not from a site visit. Nothing records the existing kitchen, so the demolition steps are general until the room is measured (Issues). No trade has seen or bid this. The days are rough working-day estimates, not quotes, and exclude waiting on orders, permits and inspectors: about 7 to 11 weeks on site once everything is bought.

What can stop the job

  • The beam. A load bearing wall stands where the island goes. Nobody removes it until the engineer has designed the beam and the permit is in hand (steps 2 and 4), and it stays shored until the beam carries the load (steps 11 to 13).
  • The slab. Find out whether it is post-tensioned before anything cuts or anchors into it (step 2). A cut cable can injure someone and damage the slab. This covers the 60" trench, the island wall's anchors and any footings for the beam's posts.
  • The floor. It isn't chosen, and it sets when the floor goes in and the height everything measures from (step 25).
  • The island wall. Its anchoring and the seven brackets need approval before it is framed, because the channels are cut in its top plate (steps 2 and 17).
  • Stone template. It can't happen until the base cabinets are set and the sink, cooktop and faucet are on site. The kitchen has no sink or counters from demolition until the stone is in.

1. Before anything is torn out

#WhoWorkBefore moving on
1OwnerMeasure the room, ceiling height in several spots, doors, windows, vents, the panel and the duct path outside. Measure the way from the living room to the kitchen for the 93" pantry and OVD3393.The 193.25" wall run, 192" island and 48" aisle fit the room.
2Structural engineer, slab scannerFind out whether the slab is post-tensioned. Scan the trench line, the island wall's anchor lines and the beam's post locations. Have the engineer set the wall's anchoring and approve the wall for the 15" overhang. Have the engineer design the beam that replaces the load bearing wall, with its posts, any footings and the temporary shoring, and say whether it sits flush in the ceiling or drops below it.Cables and pipes are marked on the slab. The beam drawings are in hand and checked against the hood, duct and ceiling lights.
3Test labThe house's age isn't recorded. If it is old enough for lead paint or asbestos, test the paint, flooring, mastic and ceiling texture.Results are in, since they can change who does the demolition.
4OwnerBid the trades and get each one's license and insurance certificate. Ask the City of Plano which permits an owner can pull and which the licensed trades must, for the electrical, plumbing and mechanical work. Ask the home insurer about the work too. Ask the city about the building permit for the beam, whether the slab cut and new walls need one too, and whether an AAV is allowed at the island sink.Permits are in hand.
5OwnerClose the Open items, choose the floor, the task pendants' bulbs, and the toe-kick tape, then order. Cabinets, the combination oven (out of stock at Bosch) and the slabs come first.Everything needed through step 34 is on site or has a confirmed date. The fridge and stools can come later.
6OwnerEmpty the kitchen and set up a temporary one, with a sink, fridge and hot plate elsewhere in the house.

2. Demolition (2 to 3 days)

#WhoWorkBefore moving on
7OwnerProtect the floors on the way to the door and through the living room. Hang dust walls and cover the HVAC returns in the work area.
8Plumber, electricianShut off, disconnect and cap the water and drains. Make the old appliance and lighting circuits safe and label them at the panel.Power and water are confirmed off in the kitchen and on elsewhere.
9Demolition crewRemove the appliances, counters, backsplash, wall and base cabinets, old hood and duct, light fixtures and, if it is being replaced, the flooring. Haul it off. Leave the load bearing wall standing until it is shored (step 11).
10Owner, plumberInspect what is exposed: wiring, plumbing, water damage. Confirm the existing plumbing really is close enough to need no trench. Snap the plan on the floor: side wall, island wall, every cabinet, 48" aisle.The owner walks the layout. Moving a line is free now.

3. Beam, slab and framing (6 to 10 days, plus any concrete cure)

#WhoWorkBefore moving on
11Structural contractorBuild the engineer's temporary shoring on both sides of the load bearing wall. Have the electrician and plumber clear anything still running through the wall.The shoring matches the drawings and the wall is clear. Nothing is cut before this.
12Structural contractor, concrete cutterRemove the wall. Cut the slab and pour any footings the engineer calls for under the beam's posts, on the marked scan.Footings are inspected before the pour and cured as the engineer says before they take load.
13Structural contractorSet the beam and posts and make the connections as drawn. Snap again any island lines the wall covered.Inspected before it is covered. The shoring comes out only when the engineer allows.
14Concrete cutterSaw-cut and dig the roughly 60" trench on the marked line.The cut matches the scan.
15ElectricianLay the conduit to the island and stub it up inside the back wall's line.Underground work is inspected before it is covered.
16Concrete cutterBackfill, patch and level the trench.Cured and flat enough for the chosen floor.
17CarpenterFrame the Coffee side wall 24" out, floor to ceiling, dead plumb, since Coffee and its uppers butt it. Frame the 192" × 34.5" island wall with its double top plate and 1/2" bracket channels, studs near 12", 27" and 72" and under each bracket, anchored as the engineer says. Add ceiling blocking for the hood and wall backing for the uppers and tall cabinets.Studs, channels and blocking are checked against Cabinets.

4. Rough-ins (3 to 5 days)

Biggest and least flexible first: duct, then drains, then wire.

#WhoWorkBefore moving on

19PlumberRun the island drain and vent in the back wall, the supply lines into the SB42, and the PEX cold water line from the attic down the wall to the recessed ice maker outlet box behind the fridge. Mock up the sink's offset drain with the disposal and the dishwasher drain loop first.Pipes clear the receptacle box 123" from the island's left end. Everything fits in the SB42. The box's shutoff valve holds with the water on.
20ElectricianPull the circuits and set the boxes from Electrical: cooktop and oven junction boxes, wall and island receptacles, the trash and sink base receptacles, fridge at 12", hood, dimmer box on the side wall, under-cabinet and glass cabinet feeds, toe-kick drivers and ceiling lights.The owner walks every box against the drawings. Moving one is cheap until the drywall is up.
21City inspectorRough electrical, plumbing, mechanical and any framing inspections.Signed off. No drywall before this.

5. Walls, ceiling and floor (6 to 10 days)

#WhoWorkBefore moving on

The floor's thickness comes off the 96" and sets the panel and riser cuts.
