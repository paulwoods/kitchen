Review 1

Design review and suggestions, 2026-10-25 · index

Overall

The design is tight and well documented. The run arithmetic checks out on both sides, work triangles are short, the cooktop has 24" landing on each side, and the trash, sink and dishwasher sit together in the right order. The suggestions below are in rough priority order. Most are about the wall run, which is where the compromises concentrate.

Biggest issues

  • The aisle is contested in exactly one spot. The oven door drops about 21" into the aisle directly across from the trash pull-out, sink and dishwasher. The dishwasher door drops about 25". Open both and the 48" aisle is almost closed, and the oven door is at shin height for anyone at the sink. Consider sliding the oven left so it faces the plates or spices drawers, which are opened briefly rather than stood at. If the oven stays, accept that it is a one-cook zone.
  • The fridge has no landing counter. The pantry beside it is 96" tall, so the nearest counter is the oven top across the filler. Swapping the pantry and the oven block would put the oven counter next to the fridge and move the oven door away from the sink in the same stroke. If the pantry must stay there, a pull-out shelf in its lower section at counter height helps.
  • The appliance garage under the W2727s does not work at full height. With 18.25" clear, the toaster oven leaves 5.5", the air fryer 3.5" and the coffee maker 2". The notes already say two of them get pulled forward to use, which means the counter is half-depth storage, not a work surface. Options: raise those three uppers to 57" or use 24"-tall W2724s, use 15"-deep uppers, or move the tall appliances to the open hutch over the oven where there is no cabinet above them. Also note the toaster oven and air fryer vent heat and steam straight up into the cabinet bottoms.
  • The microwave over the oven is cramped. A 13"-tall countertop microwave under a shelf at 51" has about 14.5" clear, and the door swing and vents sit under a shelf that holds a crock pot. Either raise the first shelf to 54" or replace the microwave with a microwave drawer in a base, which also frees the hutch.

Cooktop and hood

  • Only 3/4" of depth slack for the cooktop. The island counter is 25" and the riser face counts as a wall. The 20" cutout plus the riser and front setback leaves little room, and the riser thickness is still marked assumed. Confirm the riser build before the counter is templated, or make the island counter 26" or 27".
  • The hood is at the make-up-air limit. 600 CFM is exactly the Plano amendment ceiling. Fine if the blower is really 600 CFM, but confirm the rating with the duct run, since an island hood on a 6" duct rarely delivers its nameplate. Also the Proline sone rating is not HVI-certified, so expect it to be louder than the number.
  • The cooktop top drawer must be a false front or a shallow 3" drawer. The cutout drops 4 1/8" plus a 1" air gap. The drawings show three equal drawers. Mark the top one as false or shallow so the cabinet maker does not build a full drawer.

Electrical and plumbing

  • Island receptacle count is at the minimum. Three riser GFCIs on a 192" counter meet the one-per-island rule but leave the whole sink and dishwasher stretch without a counter outlet. Add one near the sink end, and consider dropping the horizontal orientation, which is unusual and makes the plates look like switches. The riser receptacle height of 38 7/8" is marked assumed. Confirm it clears the 42" riser top with the cover plate.
  • Disposal switch on the riser face. A toggle on a riser behind a sink gets wet hands. A counter air switch next to the faucet is the standard alternative and removes one box from the riser.
  • Island venting. The sink needs a loop vent or air admittance valve since there is no wall. This is noted in the docs but not drawn. Make sure the SB42 has room for the loop above the drain and the dishwasher high loop at the same time, with the disposal underneath.
  • Undercabinet lighting is absent. Nothing in electrical.html covers task lighting under the W2727s and the shelves over the oven. Add LED strips and a switch location while the electrician is still laying out circuits.
  • Confirm the assumed positions before ordering. The oven and cooktop junction box heights, the riser thickness, the hood body size and the disposal width are all marked assumed. Put them in the Open list of Issues until confirmed, so the cover sheet shows them.

Wall run details

  • Fridge stands 6.8" proud and sits between two 96" panels. French doors on a counter-depth fridge usually need more than 90° to pull the crisper bins, and the TEP plus pantry wall is right at the hinge. Add a 2" to 3" spacer on the hinge side or recess the fridge opening, and confirm the door swing in the top view.
  • The fillers at the oven are doing a lot of work. A 96" filler on the left and a 60" strip on the right support shelf ends. That is fine, but have the cabinet maker build them as a single hutch unit so the shelves are rigid.
  • Oven base reinforcement. The modified OW-B33-FH has its doors and shelf removed to take a 158 lb wall oven. Confirm the Bosch under-counter clearance for cooling vents, which usually require an open strip at the top of the trim, and that the counter above carries the microwave and crock pot.

Storage

  • One 18" trash pull-out with no recycling. A second bin in the same cabinet or a TRB18FH with a double bin solves it.
  • Spices in a 24" three-drawer. A 9" to 12" pull-out spice cabinet beside the cooktop would be better, or tray dividers in the top drawer.
  • The pantry lower section with three roll-out trays is good. Note the oven junction box lands behind the trays, so the electrician needs to know the tray depth.

Documentation

The sketches and cabinets.html are consistent with each other and with the decisions list. The only gap is that the "assumed" values live in the notes rather than the Issues page. Moving them to Open items would make the cover sheet an honest status of what still needs a decision.
